Idris Fenn runs the studios and marks the four checkpoints. Where the lecture
makes the case for a week's material, the studio is where it gets typed in,
gets a type error, and gets fixed — Idris spends most of a studio walking
between students who are stuck in slightly different places on the same
`match` expression.

He is the one who insists, every checkpoint, that the reference solution's
output is the specification: if your interpreter and the sample transcript
disagree on a program that isn't ambiguous in the language reference, one of
them is wrong, and it is usually not worth arguing about which without
running both. Before this course he spent a few years maintaining a
long-lived internal build tool, which is where the insistence on reproducible
output comes from.
